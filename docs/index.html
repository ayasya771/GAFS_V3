Preprocessing

Raw prices are not model input. They pass through bounded forward-fill, MAD despiking, a stationarity transform and causal volatility scaling before the network ever sees them. Each stage below is computed live from the panel you selected.

10.0

Stage 1 — despiking: erroneous prints removed, real dislocations kept

Stage 2 — log returns

Stage 3 — scaled by lagged 30-day volatility

Scaling divides each return by the realised volatility of the preceding window, never the current one, so no future information reaches the features. Macro columns are standardised with the statistics fitted on the training prefix and shipped with the model; they are applied, never refitted, which is what makes a held-out panel a fair test.

Fractional differencing

Log returns make a price series stationary but destroy its long memory. Fractional differencing with d between 0 and 1 buys stationarity while keeping some of it. Drag d to see the weight kernel and the transformed series.

Stylized facts

A generator is only useful if its output behaves like a market. This runs the model at many held-out origins and measures both the real and the generated returns with identical estimators. Nothing here is precomputed.

6

Excess kurtosis above zero and a slowly decaying autocorrelation of absolute returns are fat tails and volatility clustering. A negative leverage correlation reproduces the equity leverage effect. The mean absolute autocorrelation of raw returns should stay near zero: a generator that produces predictable returns has learned an arbitrage that does not exist.

What is running

The weights on this page are the ones the training pipeline produced. The forward pass is a direct port of the PyTorch module, and the page checks itself against a reference input and output exported from PyTorch at load time rather than asserting agreement.

Generator, conditional Temporal Fusion Transformer

    Variable selection at the current origin

    The selection network re-weights its inputs at every step. These are the average weights over the history window feeding the most recent scenario run.

    Training objective

    A Wasserstein critic with a gradient penalty, plus a contrastive head that keeps the critic's latent space organised so the generator cannot collapse onto a single safe path.

    Critic

    L_D = E[D(x_g)] - E[D(x)] + λ E[(||∇ D(x̂)||₂ - 1)²],   x̂ = εx + (1-ε)x_g,  ε ~ U[0,1]

    Contrastive term, NT-Xent over augmented views

    L_c = -log[ exp(sim(z_i, z_j)/τ) / Σ_k 1_[k≠i] exp(sim(z_i, z_k)/τ) ]

    Generator

    L_G = -E[D(x_g)] + w_u · uniformity(z_g) + w_m · MMD(z_g, z_r)

    Honest limits

    Things this demo does not claim.

    • The bundled panels are simulated, not recorded market history. They carry the right statistical structure, and the pipeline runs identically on real data, but no result here is a statement about any real instrument.
    • Volatility scaling is inverted with the volatility at the forecast origin held fixed across the horizon. Step one inverts exactly; later steps inherit that assumption.
    • The released weights come from a short CPU training run. Tail mass is thinner than the data and within-window volatility clustering is weaker than a longer schedule produces. The validation tab measures this rather than hiding it.
    • Nothing here is investment advice or a risk model you should trade against.
